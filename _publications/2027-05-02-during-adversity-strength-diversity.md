Globalization necessitates organizations to both maintain a presence in diverse countries, and engage with suppliers and customers through relevant boundary spanning enterprise systems (supply chain and customer relationship management systems). We theorize that culturally diverse organizations exhibit superior performance during adverse events due to an abundant and distributed base of knowledge, norms, and perspectives that can be leveraged for strategic change. We further posit that boundary spanning enterprise systems endow similar benefits of knowledge arbitrage opportunities, knowledge combinatorial advantages, and kaleidoscopic knowledge. Thus, we propose that a firm's diversity and its use of boundary spanning enterprise systems act as substitutes towards performance during adverse events. We test our theory by examining daily abnormal stock market returns during the Covid-19 pandemic induced stock market crash in the first quarter of 2020 for Fortune 500 firms collectively having 56,587 subsidiaries present in 179 distinct countries. We find strong and conclusive evidence through increased mean abnormal returns in cross-sectional and difference-in-differences regression specifications, with consistent results when using an alternate measure of diversity. Finally, we validate the theoretical mechanism of strategic change through post-hoc analysis of changes in resource allocation by firms. Together, these findings affirm that during adversity, strength lies in diversity.
